The Ladder · 1 of 3

Foundation: epistemics before automation.

01
The Record Is the Asset
The conversation is disposable. The record is the asset.
Try: write a resume note a stranger could restart from tomorrow, then test it by restarting cold.
02
Question-Until-Origin
Probe every failure to the origin event, with a log line as evidence.
Try: on your next bug, forbid yourself from fixing until you can name the origin event.
03
Terminal-State Prompting
Name DONE, the verification, and the danger boundary.
Try: rewrite your last three prompts so each names its stop-condition.
04
The Twice-Rule
Log every correction verbatim. A second occurrence becomes a permanent rule, same turn.
Try: keep the ledger one week, promote anything appearing twice into a written rule.
The Ladder · 2 of 3

Engine: the correction pipeline.

05
Prevent > Remind
A prose rule is the floor, not the solution.
Try: convert your most-violated rule into one mechanical check that fires without anyone remembering it.
06
Fail-Then-Pass Proof
Proof lives at the layer the user experiences, not the exit code.
Try: before trusting your next alert, force the failure it exists to catch and watch it fire.
07
Two-Key Completion
Done requires a concrete artifact: a SHA, a PR, a receipt.
Try: one full day of artifact-or-it-didn't-happen.
08
Fix the Class, Not the Instance
After any fix, hunt the estate for siblings of the same failure shape.
Try: take your last bug fix and find its siblings before calling it closed.
The Ladder · 3 of 3

Scale: what the pipeline funds.

09
Gate the Factory
Do not automate on first occurrence. Most candidates should be rejected.
Try: list five automation ideas and kill every one that fails the three-question gate.
10
Price Autonomy in Numbers
Replace approval loops with engineered safety envelopes.
Try: write one numeric boundary, enforced in code, this week.
11
Silence Must Look Like Breakage
Optimize for zero silent failures, not zero failures.
Try: pick one trusted cron job and make its silence detectable.
12
Graduate Deliberately
Route every question into a thin funnel. Force one route per thread at session end.
Try: close or file every open thread this week, graduate at most one.
Why This Becomes the Default

Manual toil doesn't compound. This does.

A person who fixes the same problem twice stays the same size. A person who turns every correction into a permanent, enforced rule gets bigger every week, without getting busier. The curriculum's own proof is that it was extracted by the method it teaches: a swarm analysis whose findings were verified against artifacts, filed as a thread, and graduated deliberately into this project.

What the ladder produces once it is running is not a smarter assistant. It is an operating layer that keeps the receipts.
